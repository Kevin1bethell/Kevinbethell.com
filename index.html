Philosophical interests

Aesthetics, embodiment, and the history of philosophy.

My primary interests are in aesthetics and philosophy of art, especially questions surrounding body art, tattooing, embodiment, artistic practice, and aesthetic experience. I am particularly interested in cases that complicate familiar distinctions between artwork and experience, artist and spectator, aesthetic value and bodily transformation.

Much of my work develops through sustained engagement with Kant, Nietzsche, and German Idealism, as well as later continental thinkers who inherit and transform aspects of these traditions, particularly Gadamer and Agamben. I am interested both in the history of aesthetic theory and in what these traditions can contribute to contemporary philosophical problems.

My broader philosophical interests include ethics, the relationship between aesthetics and ethics, asceticism, philosophy of religion, interpretation and hermeneutics, and questions of artistic agency and authorship—including those raised by AI-generated art.

A Proto-Spinozan Critique of Cartesian Dualism in 17th-Century Dutch Art

This paper explores the intersection of philosophy, religion, and art in the seventeenth-century Dutch Republic, arguing that a proto-Spinozan critique of Cartesian dualism emerges not only in the philosophical writings of the period but also within artistic transformations accompanying the Protestant Reformation. Placing Spinoza’s metaphysics alongside the evolution of Dutch painting, the paper treats the two developments as parallel products of a shared intellectual and cultural milieu...

Virum Post Machina

Could a Kantian conception of art allow AI-generated works to count as art? AI tools appear to lack qualities central to Kant’s account—freedom, subjective experience, and purposiveness. This paper argues that the human prompt-imputer can instead serve as the source of these qualities...

My master’s thesis explains Kant’s theories of art and fine art and argues that tattoos can be considered fine art within his aesthetic framework.
